Why State Taxes Matter

How much does your state affect your paycheck?

State income tax is one of the biggest variables in your take-home pay, and it varies enormously depending on where you live. Nine states — Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming — charge no state income tax on wages at all, meaning residents keep a larger share of every paycheck compared to workers in high-tax states. On the other end, states like California and New York apply progressive brackets that can reach well above 9% for higher earners. Beyond the headline rate, states also differ in standard deductions, local city or county taxes, and how bonuses or overtime are treated for withholding purposes — which is why a single national average can be misleading.

If you're comparing a job offer in a different state, or thinking about relocating, the state-specific calculators above give you an accurate side-by-side view of what you'd actually take home. For a direct comparison between two or more states, try our state tax comparison calculator.

How State Paycheck Taxes Change Take-Home Pay

State income tax can materially change the amount that reaches your bank account. Some states have no individual income tax on wages, while others use a flat rate or a progressive bracket system. A useful paycheck estimate therefore needs more than one headline percentage: deductions, exemptions, filing status and local taxes can all change the result.

Our dedicated state calculators use 2026 federal brackets plus state-specific rules that have been checked against government tax guidance. The estimate also includes Social Security and Medicare. City, county, school-district, reciprocity, special credits and unusual wage situations may not be included, so use the result for planning rather than as a tax return or payroll statement.
